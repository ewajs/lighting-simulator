# 0043. Fixture models: a model provider and glTF conventions

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-12

## Context

Luminaires were drawn as generic placeholders per mount type: a 1.2 m
linear pendant looked like a round disc. Clients have real models of their
products, which their manufacturers supply. Core never fetches (ADR 0002),
and IES files already come through a provider (ADR 0034).

## Options

- **What the provider returns:** the file's bytes, or a URL for core to load
- **Demo models:** generated by us at each product's approximate size; free
  CC0 models that don't match the products; or manufacturer files, usually
  Revit or 3DS, which would need converting

## Decision

- **`ModelProvider.load(productId) → Promise<ArrayBuffer | undefined>`** on
  `SimulatorOptions.models`, returning a binary glTF (`.glb`).
  - `Simulator` caches each product's file and only checks the glTF magic.
    `simulator.model(id)` and `onModel` work like `photometry` and `onPhotometry`.
  - The renderer parses with three's `GLTFLoader` (part of three, no new
    dependency), once per file, and clones a template per luminaire.
  - A missing model, a failed load, a file that isn't `.glb`, or a model more
    than 6 m across (probably not in metres) gives a `console.warn` and the
    placeholder.
- **Model conventions,** which clients prepare their files to:
  - Metres, Y up, **origin at the mounting point**: the ceiling cut-out
    (recessed, track), the top of the pendant, the wall plate (wall), or the
    floor (floor, table).
  - **Front towards +Z.** This is the IES C0 plane, along a linear luminaire,
    so the light and the model turn together. For wall fixtures, +Z points into
    the room.
  - Optional named parts:
    - `light`: where the light sits. Default: the origin.
    - `head`: the part that turns with the aim, with no rotation of its own.
    - `cord…` nodes: where pendant cords attach, each stretched up to the
      ceiling by core. Default: one at the origin.
    - Materials named `emissive…`: glow with the luminaire's colour and dimming.
  - Fixtures cast no shadows, as before, so the light isn't trapped by its own
    housing.
- **Pan turns the whole fixture unless it has a `head`.** It's the fixture's
  installed orientation, so an asymmetric model (a wall washer's kicker, a
  linear profile) follows its light. Tilt stays the light's. The lighting
  is unchanged: it's the same rotation applied one level up.
- **Picking with a tolerance:** a click or tap within 10 px of a fixture's
  body on screen picks it. A thin linear profile is otherwise very hard to
  tap on a phone.
- **Demo models are generated by us.** `apps/demo/scripts/build-models.js`
  builds simple models at each IES product's approximate size, so they
  carry no third-party rights. `public/models/SOURCES.md` says what they
  are. The generic products keep the placeholder, which shows the fallback.

## Consequences

- New public API: `ModelProvider`, `SimulatorOptions.models`,
  `Simulator.model` / `onModel`, and `ModelListener`.
- Clients supply real models through their backend, as with IES files. The
  demo's models only show the mechanism.
- A model's geometry is shared by all luminaires of the product. Only its
  emissive materials are copied per luminaire.
